Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

Metaheuristic Optimization Inspired by Bird Flocking and Fish Schooling

Biological Inspiration

Imagine a flock of birds randomly searching for a single piece of food in an area. None of the birds know exactly where the food is, but in every iteration, they know how far away it is. The optimal strategy? **Follow the bird closest to the food.**

Developed by James Kennedy and Russell Eberhart in 1995, **Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)** replicates this collective choreographic intelligence using simple individual vectors (particles) that adapt based on personal and community success:

Personal Best (Cognitive)

Each particle remembers the location where it personally achieved its best performance (highest fitness) so far. This represents its individual memory/experience.

Global Best (Social)

The entire swarm shares information to identify the absolute best location found by *any* particle in the colony. This drives social learning and swarm-wide alignment.

Velocity Alignment

Particles continuously adjust their flight speed and direction to balance their current inertia, their pull toward their personal best, and their pull toward the global best.

Mathematical Formulation

Each candidate solution in the multi-dimensional search space is represented as a **Particle** with a position vector \(\vec{x}_i\) and a velocity vector \(\vec{v}_i\).

1. Velocity Update Equation

At each time step, the velocity of the \(i\)-th particle in dimension \(d\) is updated by balancing three forces:

$$v_{id}^{t+1} = w v_{id}^t + c_1 r_1 (p_{id}^t - x_{id}^t) + c_2 r_2 (g_d^t - x_{id}^t)$$

Where:
• \(v_{id}^t\) is the current velocity of particle \(i\) in dimension \(d\).
• \(w\) is the **inertia weight**, controlling how much of the previous flight direction is preserved.
• \(c_1\) is the **cognitive coefficient** (self-confidence), pulling the particle toward its personal best.
• \(c_2\) is the **social coefficient** (peer influence), pulling the particle toward the global best.
• \(r_1, r_2\) are independent random numbers generated uniformly in the range \([0, 1]\) at each step.
• \(p_{id}^t\) is the personal best position (\(pbest\)) of particle \(i\).
• \(g_d^t\) is the global best position (\(gbest\)) found in the entire swarm.
• \(x_{id}^t\) is the current position of particle \(i\).

2. Position Update Equation

Using the newly calculated velocity vector, each particle flies to its new position in the search space:

$$x_{id}^{t+1} = x_{id}^t + v_{id}^{t+1}$$

Algorithm Steps & Pseudocode

The operational framework of the standard PSO loop is structured as follows:

Initialize a population of particles with random positions \(x_i\) and velocities \(v_i\).
Evaluate fitness for each particle.
Set personal bests \(p_i = x_i\) and find global best \(g\).

while (iteration < max_iterations) {
    for each Particle \(i\):
        // 1. Update velocity and fly
        Update velocity \(v_i\) using the three components (Inertia, Cognitive, Social).
        Update position \(x_i = x_i + v_i\).
        
        // 2. Evaluate fitness
        Evaluate new fitness \(f(x_i)\).
        
        // 3. Update Personal Best
        if \(f(x_i)\) is better than \(f(p_i)\):
            \(p_i = x_i\)
            
        // 4. Update Global Best
        if \(f(x_i)\) is better than \(f(g)\):
            \(g = x_i\)

    Save/Log global best \(g\).
}

Key Parameters

Inertia Weight (\(w\))

Balances global exploration and local exploitation. A high inertia weight (\(> 0.9\)) promotes exploration of new areas, whereas a small weight (\(< 0.4\)) focuses the swarm on exploiting the immediate local neighborhood.

Cognitive Weight (\(c_1\))

Determines how much a particle relies on its own memory. High values lead to particles wandering around their individual historical bests, causing wide exploration paths.

Social Weight (\(c_2\))

Determines how much a particle responds to the swarm's success. High values pull all particles rapidly toward the global best, accelerating convergence but risking local optima traps.

Why PSO is Highly Effective

PSO does not use traditional evolutionary operators (such as selection, mutation, or crossover). It relies purely on continuous position-shifting vector kinematics. This makes it extremely fast to compute, highly robust for continuous optimization, and simple to implement in multi-dimensional spaces.
