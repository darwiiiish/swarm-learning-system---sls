Artificial Bee Colony (ABC)

Metaheuristic Optimization Inspired by Honeybee Foraging Behavior

Biological Inspiration

In nature, honeybee colonies organize themselves with incredible coordination to find the highest-concentration nectar sources. The Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm, proposed by Derviş Karaboğa in 2005, replicates this collective intelligence by partitioning the colony into three classes of bees, each with specialized search responsibilities:

Employed Bees

Employed bees are assigned to specific, active food sources. They gather nectar information, communicate this information back to the hive, and explore neighboring regions in search of better food sources.

Onlooker Bees

Onlooker bees wait in the hive at the "dance floor" and choose food sources to explore further. Their choice is based on probability, favoring richer food sources shared by Employed bees through waggle dances.

Scout Bees

When a food source is exhausted (exceeding a pre-defined depletion limit without improvement), its Employed bee abandons the source and becomes a Scout bee to search randomly for brand new food sources.

Mathematical Formulation

The search space corresponds to the foraging area, food sources correspond to candidate solutions, and the nectar amount matches the fitness value of the solution.

1. Exploring Neighboring Food Sources

Both Employed and Onlooker bees generate a new candidate source \(v_{ij}\) in the neighborhood of their assigned source \(x_{ij}\) using the following formula:

$$v_{ij} = x_{ij} + \phi_{ij}(x_{ij} - x_{kj})$$

Where:
• \(i\) is the index of the selected food source.
• \(j\) is a randomly chosen parameter dimension (\(j \in \{1, 2, ..., D\}\)).
• \(k\) is a randomly selected food source index different from \(i\).
• \(\phi_{ij}\) is a random scaling factor generated uniformly in the interval \([-1, 1]\).

2. Fitness Evaluation & Selection

A greedy selection is made between the parent \(x_i\) and candidate \(v_i\). The fitness \(fit_i\) of a solution (minimization problem) is calculated as:

$$fit_i = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{1 + f_i} & \text{if } f_i \ge 0 \\ 1 + |f_i| & \text{if } f_i < 0 \end{cases}$$

Where \(f_i\) is the objective function value of the candidate food source.

3. Waggle Dance Selection Probability

An Onlooker bee selects a food source \(i\) based on its probability \(P_i\), proportional to the nectar quality:

$$P_i = \frac{fit_i}{\sum_{n=1}^{SN} fit_n}$$

Where \(SN\) is the total number of food sources (equal to half of the total colony size).

4. Scout Bee Initialization

If a position \(x_i\) cannot be improved for a predetermined number of cycles (the **Limit** parameter), it is abandoned, and a new food source is initialized by the Scout bee:

$$x_{ij} = x_{min, j} + \text{rand}(0,1)(x_{max, j} - x_{min, j})$$

Algorithm Steps & Pseudocode

The standard operational loop of the ABC optimizer is structured as follows:

Initialize all food sources \(x_i\) and evaluate fitness.
while (iteration < max_iterations) {
    // 1. Employed Bees Phase
    for each Employed Bee \(i\):
        Produce candidate food source \(v_i\) using neighborhood search.
        Apply greedy selection between \(x_i\) and \(v_i\).
        if no improvement: increment trial counter \(t_i\).
        else: reset trial counter \(t_i = 0\).

    // 2. Onlooker Bees Phase
    Calculate selection probability \(P_i\) for all sources.
    for each Onlooker Bee:
        Choose food source \(i\) based on probability \(P_i\).
        Produce candidate food source \(v_i\) and apply greedy selection.
        Update trial counter \(t_i\) accordingly.

    // 3. Scout Bees Phase
    if any trial counter \(t_i > \text{Limit}\):
        Replace food source \(i\) with a random position.
        Reset trial counter \(t_i = 0\).

    Save the best food source found so far.
}

Key Parameters

Bee Population

Total colony size. It is divided equally into Employed Bees (\(SN\)) and Onlooker Bees (\(SN\)). A larger population increases search coverage but increases computational overhead per step.

Limit (Depletion Threshold)

Determines how many attempts are allowed without progress before the source is abandoned. A low limit forces rapid exploration (scouting), while a high limit allows deeper local exploitation.

Speed Factor

Adjusts the learning rate/velocity modifier of the swarm in simulations, altering how quickly bees adjust positions based on the random coefficient \(\phi_{ij}\).

Exploration vs. Exploitation Balance

The separation of Employed bees (local searching) and Onlooker bees (quality-proportional exploration) gives ABC a remarkably strong ability to balance global search and local refinement, avoiding local optima traps via the random Scouting phase.
